Spec Kit Extension

Prove it, then build it.

Keel is a guided discovery layer for Spec Kit. It won't let an idea reach a spec until real interviews back it up — a script that actually blocks, not a chatbot that agrees with you. Once it does, Keel hands off to Spec Kit's own pipeline to build the thing for real, and checks what shipped against what you learned.

Try the flow ↓ Install →

Keel doesn't write your code, and it won't pad evidence to make its own gate pass. It just won't let you skip finding out if you're right.

Built for the moment right before you'd normally just start typing into /speckit.specify. If you've got an idea and aren't sure it's worth building, Keel walks you through finding out — then carries you straight into Spec Kit to make it real.

Why

Nothing upstream checks whether the idea deserved a spec

Spec Kit is very good at turning a specification into working software. It has no opinion on whether the specification should have existed — that's a different problem, and it's the one Keel solves.

problem 1

Assumptions become spec

Whatever you type into /speckit.specify becomes the plan. There's no step before it that asks whether the belief behind the feature was ever checked against anyone who isn't you.

problem 2

clarify asks you, not evidence

/speckit.clarify resolves ambiguity by asking the person who already believes the idea. Your assumptions become the spec either way. Keel resolves ambiguity against interviews instead.

problem 3

Nothing checks the build against why

/speckit.analyze checks whether your spec, plan, and tasks agree with each other. Nothing checks whether what shipped agrees with the evidence that justified building it. That's what keel.audit is for.

How it works

One command to guide you, five if you already know the shape

Four commands run before /speckit.specify, one runs after your build is done. None of them replace a Spec Kit command — they surround it. You don't have to sequence them yourself or track A-00X / E-00X IDs — /speckit.keel.guide reads your project's state and does that.

→

/speckit.keel.guide

Run it with no arguments. It figures out whether you need a hypothesis, an interview, a check, or a brief — and does that step inline, in plain language, referring to your assumptions and evidence by what they actually say rather than by ID. That's the whole interface: one command, start to finish.

What guide actually runs underneath — five phases
keel.init

Captures the hypothesis, derives assumptions, writes a non-leading interview guide and an evidence-gathering plan — who to talk to, and why.

keel.add-evidence

Ingests one interview, extracts claims with provenance, updates assumption confidence.

keel.check

Reports coverage, saturation, and contradictions, then a five-option decision menu: pivot, gather more evidence, reduce risk, narrow the hypothesis, or proceed.

keel.brief

Writes constitution input and an evidence-backed brief for /speckit.specify.

keel.audit

Scores spec quality; with keel/ present, diffs the build against the evidence.

Each one also works as a direct command — /speckit.keel.init, /speckit.keel.check, and so on — if you already know exactly which phase you're in. guide is a router around them, not a replacement.

The gate actually enforces

keel-gate.sh runs at the top of every Keel command. /speckit.keel.brief is blocked while any high-risk assumption is unvalidated, evidence is thin, or all of it comes from a single participant role — not as a suggestion to your agent, as an exit code.

Thresholds live in keel-config.yml. You can lower them; the override gets recorded so keel.audit can surface it later.

$ bash keel-gate.sh brief evidence files: 2 distinct participant roles: 2 unvalidated high-risk: 1 contradictions: 0 BLOCKED: only 2 evidence files, minimum is 3. -> Run /speckit.keel.add-evidence after more interviews. BLOCKED: 1 high-risk assumption(s) still unvalidated. -> Validate them, downgrade the risk with a reason, or add 'override: A-XXX' to keel/decisions.md.
Install

Add it to a Spec Kit project

Requires Python 3.11+. Pick whichever matches where you're starting from — Keel is a Spec Kit extension, so it needs Spec Kit either way.

Spec Kit project already initialized (specify init)
Spec Kit CLI ≥ 0.15.0
Python 3.11+ on your PATH
bash, git, awk, sed, grep (already on macOS/Linux)
1

Install the extension

specify extension add keel --from https://github.com/keeldiscovery/spec-kit-keel/archive/refs/heads/main.zip

Not in Spec Kit's bundled catalog yet, so the bare specify extension add keel won't resolve — the command above installs straight from this repo. Spec Kit will ask you to confirm the external source (y) once. Developing locally instead? specify extension add --dev /path/to/spec-kit-keel

2

Verify it installed cleanly

./tests/test-install.sh
3

Confirm the commands are live

/speckit.keel.guide
/speckit.keel.init
/speckit.keel.add-evidence
/speckit.keel.check
/speckit.keel.brief
/speckit.keel.audit

These show up alongside your existing /speckit.* commands in your coding agent — nothing else in your workflow changes. Start with /speckit.keel.guide if you'd rather be walked through it; the other five are there directly whenever you want them.

Try it — no install required

Walk one discovery cycle end to end

A real product idea, a real (short) interview guide, and three interviews you reveal one at a time. Watch the assumptions table, the gate, and the recommendation change after each one — this is exactly what keel-gate.sh computes for real, including where it blocks.

the idea

TeamPulse Digest

A Slack bot for engineering managers that reads standup threads and drafts a weekly digest of progress, blockers, and risk — with a mandatory human-review step before anything sends. Underlying belief: managers lose real time every week reconstructing team status by hand, and a reviewed draft would recover it without adding risk.

keel/brief.md

Evidence-backed problem statement

Managers spend real, recurring weekly time reconstructing team status by hand (A-001, E-001/E-003).

What to build

A weekly digest with a mandatory human-review step before send — not a nice-to-have, the specific mechanism the evidence says makes an AI-authored summary acceptable at all (A-002, E-001/E-002/E-003). keel: A-001, A-002

Residual risk carried into build

A-002 cleared the gate at "supported," not "validated," on exactly two roles. One of the two engineering managers (E-002) trusted the review step only conditionally — treat initial rollout trust as earned per manager, not solved by shipping the review button.

Or run it for real

Everything above is exactly what these five commands would produce. Paste them in order, in your own project, once the extension is installed — or paste just /speckit.keel.init's argument into /speckit.keel.guide and stay in one conversation; it runs the same five phases for you and tells you when to paste the next interview.

/speckit.keel.init "TeamPulse Digest: a Slack bot for engineering managers that reads standup threads and drafts a weekly digest of progress, blockers, and risk, with a mandatory human-review step before anything sends. Belief: managers lose real time every week reconstructing team status by hand."

/speckit.keel.add-evidence "Participant: engineering_manager (EM-1). Q: Walk me through the last time you told your manager what your team worked on last week. A: Every Friday I go through six different threads to figure out what shipped — it's an hour, every week. Also: if a bot drafted that and I could fix the two things it got wrong, I'd take that trade."

/speckit.keel.add-evidence "Participant: engineering_manager (EM-2). Q: If someone handed you a draft summary of your team's week, what would you do with it before passing it along? A: If I'm the one who has to sign off, sure, I'd trust that more than something that fires off on its own. I might still double-check the first few, until I trust it."

/speckit.keel.add-evidence "Participant: director (DIR-1). Q: How do you feel about a machine drafting your team's status report? A: As long as my EM has read it and signed off, I don't care who wrote the first draft. The review step is the whole thing for me."
